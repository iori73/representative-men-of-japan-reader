ESSAY 03

Ninomiya Sontoku — A Peasant Saint

二宮尊徳 — 農聖

一、今世紀初頭の日本農業 I. Japanese Agriculture in the Beginning of the Present Century

農業は国家存立の基盤である。 Agriculture is the ground-basis of the national existence;

わが国のような、海運や通商の利に恵まれていても、 essentially so in a country like ours, where, despite all its maritime and commercial advantages,

民の暮らしを支える主なものはやはり土地である。 the main support of the people comes from its soil.

天然の肥沃さだけでは、これほど狭い面積にこれほど多くの人口を支えることはできない。 Natural fertility alone cannot support so immense a population upon so limited an area,

十五万平方マイルに四千八百万の民、しかも耕作可能な地はその二割に過ぎない。 48,000,000 upon 150,000 sq. miles, only 20 per cent of which are cultivable.

土地には限界まで実りを出させねばならず、 The land must be made to yield its maximum,

そのために人の知恵と勤勉を出し尽くさねばならない。 and human genius and industry must be exerted to the utmost for that end.

日本の農業は、世界でも類を見ないほど見事なものだと私たちは考えている。 We consider Japanese agriculture to be the most remarkable of the kind in the world.

一塊の土にも心のこもった手入れが施され、 Every clod of earth receives thoughtful manipulation,

地から芽吹くひとつひとつの若苗にも、親の情愛にも似た世話が注がれる。 and to every plantlet that starts from the ground is given a care and attention well nigh bordering upon parental affection.

学の足りぬところは、ひたむきな勤勉さで補ってきた。 The science we lacked in we supplied with strenuous industry,

その結果、千三百万エーカーの耕地を、市場向け菜園さながらの精緻さで保っているのである。 and as a result we have 13,000,000 acres of cultivated surface, kept with all the nicety and perfection of market-gardens.

これほどの高度な耕作は、人々の並外れた勤勉あってこそ可能になる。 Such a high degree of cultivation is possible only by more than ordinary industry on the people's part.

わずかな怠りが、たちまち見るも無残な荒廃を招く。 A little negligence is sure to call in desolation of the most unattractive character.

人手に見捨てられた田畑ほど、気を滅入らせるものを私たちは知らない。 We know of nothing so disheartening as a once cultivated field abandoned by human labor.

原生林の力強さも豊かさも持たぬまま、見捨てられた田は暗い絶望の色をたたえる。 Without the vigor and luxuriance of the primitive forest, the desolation of the deserted field is that of black despair.

処女地を切り拓こうとする者は十人いても、荒れ地を取り戻そうとする者は一人もいない。 For ten men that would dare to break up the virgin soil, not one will apply himself to recover the abandoned land.

新大陸が勤勉な国々の民を招き寄せる一方で、バビロンは今もふくろうと蠍の棲み処のままである。 While the Americas invite the thrifty nations of the world, Babylon remains as a habitation of owls and scorpions.

十九世紀の初め、日本の農業はまことに嘆かわしい状態にあった。 In the beginning of the nineteenth century, Japanese agriculture was in a most lamentable state.

二百年におよぶ太平の世は、あらゆる階層に贅沢と放埒をもたらし、 The long-continued peace of two-hundred years brought in luxuries and dissipation among men of all classes,

そこから生まれた怠惰は、たちまち耕地に影を落とした。 and indolence thus introduced had immediate effects upon the cultivated fields.

土地の多くで年貢の実りは三分の二も減った。 In many places, the revenue from land decreased by two-thirds.

あざみと藪が、かつて豊かだった田畑を侵し、 Thistles and bushes invaded the once productive fields,

わずかに残った耕地には、なお領主への年貢のすべてがのしかかった。 and what little was left in cultivation had to bear all the feudal dues levied from the land.

村という村が、見渡すかぎりの荒廃の相を呈していた。 Village after village bore an aspect of utter desolation.

実直な労働があまりに苦しくなり、人々は不実な道へと逃れていった。 Honest labor becoming too onerous, men betook themselves to dishonest ways.

恵み深い大地から尽きせぬ恵みを求めることをやめ、 From the kind earth they ceased to look for her ever bounteous gifts,

互いを欺き騙し合うことで、命をつなぐわずかなものを得ようとした。 and by cheating and defrauding one another they sought to acquire what little they needed to sustain their ill-doomed existence.

すべての災いの根は道徳にあった。天は、その卑しい子らに報いることを拒み、 The whole cause of their evils was moral, and Nature refusing to reward her ignoble sons,

この地に降りかかったあらゆる不幸を招いたのである。 brought about all the miseries that befell the land.

そのとき、天の理と心を通わせるひとりの男が生まれた。 Then was born a man whose spirit was in league with Nature's laws.

二、少年時代 II. Boyhood

二宮金次郎、号して尊徳(徳を敬う者)は、天明七年(一七八七年)に生まれた。 Ninomiya Kinjiro, surnamed Sontok (Admirer of Virtue), was born in the seventh year of Tenmei (1787).

父は相模国の名も知れぬ村に住む、貧しい小百姓だったが、 His father was a farmer of very small means in an obscure village in the province of Sagami,

その慈悲深さと公共の心においては近隣に知られていた。 notable, however, among his neighbors for his charity and public spirit.

十六のとき、尊徳は二人の幼い弟とともに孤児となり、 At the age of 16, Sontok, with his two little brothers, was orphaned,

親族の話し合いの末、貧しい一家は離散させられることとなった。 and the conference of his relatives decided upon the dissolution of his poor family,

長男である彼は、父方の伯父のもとに引き取られた。 and he, the eldest, was placed under the custody of one of his paternal uncles.

そこで少年は、伯父にできるだけ厄介をかけまいと心を尽くした。 Here the lad's whole endeavor was to be as little burdensome to his uncle as possible.

一人前の働きができぬことを嘆き、日中にできなかった分を取り戻そうと、 He lamented that he could not do a man's part, and to make up what he in his youth could not accomplish in daytime,

夜更けまで働き続けた。 he would work till very late at midnight.

やがて彼の胸に、無学のまま、先人の知恵に対して「開いた盲」のような大人にはなるまい、という思いが芽生えた。 Then came a thought to him that he would not grow up to be an illiterate man, an "open-blind" to the wisdoms of ancients.

そこで彼は孔子『大学』の写本を手に入れ、一日の労働を終えた夜更けに、 So he procured a copy of Confucius' Great Learning, and in the depth of night after the day's full work,

熱心にその古典を学び始めた。 he applied himself assiduously to his classic study.

だがまもなく伯父は勉学中の彼を見つけ、 But soon his uncle found him at his study,

何の益にもならぬ勉強のために貴重な油を使ったと、きつく叱りつけた。 sharply reprimanded him for the use of precious oil for work from which he (the uncle) could not derive any benefit,

伯父には、その若者自身にとっても実際的な益があるとは思えなかったのである。 and could see no practical good to the youth himself.

尊徳は伯父の憤りをもっともだと思い、自分の油を持てるようになるまで勉強をあきらめた。 Sontok considered his uncle's resentment reasonable, and gave up his study till he could have oil of his own to burn.

そこで翌春、川岸の誰のものでもない小さな土地を切り拓き、 So the next spring, he broke up a little land that belonged to nobody, on the bank of a river,

そこに菜種を植え、休日のすべてをこの自分だけの作物のために注いだ。 and there planted some rape-seed and gave all of his holidays to the raising of this crop of his own.

一年の終わりには、自らの手で育てた種を大袋いっぱいに収め、 At the end of one year, he had a large bagful of the seed, the product of his own hand,

正直な労働への報いとして、天から直に受け取ったのだった。 and received directly from Nature as a reward of his honest labor.

その種を近くの油屋に持ち込み、数ガロンの油と換えてもらうと、 He took the seed to a neighbouring oil-factory, had it exchanged for a few gallons of the oil,

これでもう伯父の蔵から借りずに勉強を続けられると、言葉にできぬほど喜んだ。 and was glad beyond expression that he could now resume his study without drawing from his uncle's store.

意気揚々と夜の勉学に戻った彼は、その忍耐と勤勉を伯父に褒めてもらえるのではという淡い期待も抱いていた。 Triumphantly he returned to his night-lesson, not without some hope of words of applause from his uncle for patience and industry such as his.

ところがそうではなかった。伯父は、お前を養っている以上その時間もまた自分のものだ、 But no! the uncle said that the youth's time was also his, seeing that he supported him,

読書などという実入りのない仕事に手の者を使わせるわけにはいかぬと言った。 and that he could not afford to let any of his men engage in so unprofitable a work as book-reading.

尊徳はまたも伯父の言い分をもっともだと思い、その命に従って、 Sontok again thinks his uncle is reasonable, follows his behest,

農作業を終えたあとは、莚編みや草履作りに励んだ。 and goes to mat-weaving and sandal-making after the day's heavy work upon the farm is done.

それ以来、彼の学びは、伯父の家のために毎日薪や秣を取りに通う山道の行き帰りで続けられた。 Since then, his studies were prosecuted on his way to and from hills whereunto he was daily sent to fetch hay and fuel for his uncle's household.

休日だけは彼自身のものであり、それを遊興に浪費するような人物ではなかった。 His holidays were his, and he was not one to throw them away for amusements.

菜種での経験は、地道な労働の価値を彼に教え、より大きな規模でそれを試したいと願うようになった。 His experiment with the rape-seed taught him the value of earnest labor, and he wished to renew his experiment upon a larger scale.

村の中に、先ごろの洪水で沼地に変わった一角を見つけ、そこで休日を役立てる格好の機会を得た。 He found in his village a spot changed into a marsh-pond by a recent flood, wherein was a capital opportunity for him to employ his holidays for useful purposes.

沼の水を抜き、底をならして、こぢんまりとした田を仕立てた。 He drained the pond, levelled its bottom, and prepared it for a snug little rice-field.

そこに、農家が普段は捨てるような余り苗を選んで植え、夏の間じゅう心を込めて世話をした。 There he planted some seedlings that he picked out of the surplus usually cast away by farmers, and bestowed upon them a summer's watchful care.

秋には黄金の稲一俵(二ブッシェル)がもたらされ、生まれて初めて自らの慎ましい努力への報いとして糧を得た孤児の少年の喜びは、察するに余りある。 The autumn brought him a bagful (2 bushels) of golden grain, and we can imagine the joy of our orphan-boy who for the first time in his life had his life-stuff provided him as a reward for his humble effort.

その秋に得た収穫こそ、彼の波乱に満ちた生涯の元手となった。 The crop he gathered that autumn was the fund upon which he started his eventful career.

まことに独立した人であった。 True, independent man was he!

彼は、天が誠実に働く者に対して裏切らぬことを学び、 He learnt that Nature is faithful to honest sons of toil,

後年のあらゆる改革は皆、天の理に従う者には天が豊かに報いるという、この単純な原理の上に築かれた。 and all his subsequent reforms were based upon this simple principle that Nature rewardeth abundantly them that obey her laws.

数年後、彼は伯父の家を出て、 A few years afterward he left his uncle's house,

村で見つけて改良した荒れ地からわずかに得た穀物を手に、 and with what little grain he gathered with his own hand out of the mere refuse lands he discovered and improved in his village,

長らく打ち捨てられていた父祖の家に戻った。 he returned to his paternal cottage, now deserted for many years.

その忍耐と信念と勤勉の前には、混沌と荒廃を秩序と実りへと変えようとする試みを阻むものは何もなかった。 With his patience, faith, and industry, nothing stood in his way in his attempt to convert chaos and desolation into order and productivity.

山の斜面も、川岸の荒れ地も、道端も、沼地も、すべてが彼に富と実りをもたらし、 Declivities of the hills, wastes on river-banks, roadsides, marshes, all added wealth and substance to him,

幾年もせぬうちに、彼は相応の資産を持ち、その倹約と勤勉の模範ゆえに近隣一帯の尊敬を集める人物となっていた。 and before many years he was a man of no little means, respected by his entire neighborhood for his exemplary economy and industry.

彼はあらゆるものを自らの力で克服し、他の者たちが同じように己を克服する手助けをする用意もできていた。 He conquered all things for himself, and he was ready to help others to make similar conquests for themselves.

三、その手腕が試されるとき III. The Test of His Ability

彼の名声は日ごとに高まり、その値打ちは小田原藩主の知るところとなった。 His fame daily increasing, his worth was recognized by the Lord of Odawara, whose subject he was,

尊徳はその家臣であり、藩主は当時の将軍家の老中として、国内でも並ぶ者なき権勢を振るっていた。 and who as the then Prime-Minister of the Tycoon's Government, wielded an influence second to none in the Empire.

これほどの人材を、村の片隅に埋もれさせておくわけにはいかなかった。 So valuable a subject was not to be left buried in the obscurity of village life;

だが身分の隔てがこれほど強い当時の世にあって、一介の百姓が要職に登用されるには、 but in the society of his time, when class-distinctions were so strong, the promotion of a peasant to any position of influence was possible only when he gave unmistakable evidence of extraordinary ability,

そうした慣例破りに対して必ず起こるであろう世の反発を封じるに足るだけの、非凡な力の証しが要った。 enough to silence popular protest that was sure to be brought against any such infraction of regular social routine.

この目的のために選ばれた仕事は、尊徳ほどの不屈の忍耐を持つ者でなければ、誰もが落胆するような代物だった。 The job that was selected for this purpose was of most disappointing nature to any but one of Sontok's indomitable patience.

小田原藩の領地のうち、下野国にある物井・横田・東郷の三村は、 Among the feudal possessions of the Lord of Odawara were the three villages of Monoi, Yokota, and Tosho in the province of Shimotzuke,

幾世代もの放置によって、恐るべき荒廃に陥っていた。 which, through the neglect of several generations, had gone into fearful desolation.

かつてこの三村には四百五十戸を数え、年貢として四千俵(八千ブッシェル)の米を領主に納めていた。 The three villages once counted 450 families, and tendered as their annual feudal dues 4000 bags (8000 bushels) of rice to their rightful Lord.

だが今や荒野が田畑を侵し、狢と狐が人と住処を分け合うありさまで、 But now that wild Nature invaded their fields, and badgers and foxes shared habitations with men,

人口はかつての三分の一にまで減り、貧しい百姓から取り立てられるのはせいぜい八百俵だった。 the population numbered only one-third of what it had been, and 800 bags were the utmost that could be levied from the impoverished farmers.

貧しさとともに道徳も廃れ、かつて勤勉だった村々は今や博打打ちの巣窟と化していた。 With poverty came moral degradation, and the once thrifty villages were now dens of gamblers.

その再興は幾度も試みられたが、村人自身が根っからの盗人と怠け者である以上、金も権威も何の役にも立たなかった。 Their restoration was attempted several times; but neither money nor authority was of any avail when the villagers themselves were confirmed thieves and idlers.

より楽観的な領主であれば、住民をすべて退去させ、より徳ある新たな働き手を移住させることで、 A more sanguine master might have determined upon the withdrawal of the entire population, and by the importation of new and more virtuous labor,

怠惰な旧民が荒らした田畑の回復に着手していたかもしれない。 might have begun to recover the fields left desolate by his indolent subjects.

しかしこの村々は、何の役にも立たぬがゆえにこそ、小田原藩主の思惑にはうってつけだった。 But, these villages, if good for no purpose, just served the purpose which the Lord of Odawara had in view.

これらの村を元の富と繁栄に戻せる者になら、国内の(数多くあった)荒れ村すべての再興を託せるだろうし、 A man that could restore these villages to their original wealth and prosperity might be entrusted with the restoration of all deserted villages (of which there were a great many) in the country;

これまで誰もが失敗した仕事に成功した者は、世に正当な指導者として示され、 and he that succeeded where all before him had failed might be brought before the public as their rightful leader,

士族らの不満を恐れることなく、しかるべき権限を与えられるはずだった。 and be clothed with proper authority without fear of discontent from the titled classes.

こうして主君は、この仕事を引き受けるよう尊徳に迫った。 This was the job then which Sontok was prevailed upon by his master to undertake.

この百姓は、己の卑しい生まれと、これほど公の仕事には到底不向きだという理由でその栄誉を辞退した。 The peasant declined the honor upon the ground of his humble birth and his total inability for a work of so public a nature;

彼はしがない土いじりの身であり、生涯に望みうる最大のことといえば、自らの才覚ではなく先祖の功徳によって、自分の家産を立て直すことだけだった。 he a poor tiller of the soil, and the utmost he expected to accomplish in his life was the restoration of his own family-property, and that not by his ability, but by the inherited merits of his ancestors.

三年の長きにわたり、藩主は家臣たる彼への要求を押し通そうとし、 For three long years the Lord insisted in his demand from his subject,

尊徳もまた同じだけ頑なに、謙遜と、自らの藁屋根の下での平穏な暮らしを願い続けた。 who as persistently maintained his modesty and request for peaceful domesticity under his own thatched roof.

だがついに、敬愛する主君の懇請にもはや抗いきれなくなったとき、 When, however, the importunities of his worthy superior were no longer to be resisted,

尊徳は、自らが再興すべき村々の実情を子細に調べる許しを求めた。 Sontok asked for permission to carefully examine the situation of the villages he was to revive.

彼は自らの足で百三十マイルの道を歩いてそこへ赴き、幾月も人々のもとにとどまり、 Thither he went upon his own feet, a distance of 130 miles, and for months remained among the people,

一軒一軒を訪ねては、その暮らしぶりを丹念に観察した。 visiting them from house to house, and carefully watching their ways of living;

土質、荒れ地の広がり、排水、灌漑の可能性などをつぶさに調べ、 made a close study of the nature of the soil, the extent of wilderness, drainage, possible means of irrigation, etc.,

荒れ果てた地域を再興しうるかどうかの、全体の見積もりを立てるためのあらゆる材料を集めた。 and gathered all the data for making his full estimate for the possible restoration of the deserted district.

小田原藩主への報告は、まことに心細いものだった。だがこの一件は、まるごと諦めるべきものでもなかった。 His report to the Lord of Odawara was most discouraging; but the case was not one to be wholly given up.

「仁愛の道だけが、あの貧しい人々に平安と豊かさを取り戻させることができます」と彼は報告に記した。 "The art of love alone can restore peace and abundance to those poor people," said he in his report.

「金銭の給付も、年貢の免除も、彼らの苦境を救う助けには少しもなりません。 "Grants in money, or release from taxes, will in no way help them in their distress.

実のところ、彼らを救う秘訣のひとつは、あらゆる金銭的援助を差し控えることにあるのです。 Indeed, one secret of their salvation lies in withdrawing all monetary help from them.

そうした援助は、ただ強欲と怠惰を誘うばかりで、人々の間に不和の種を蒔くだけです。 Such help only induces avarice and idleness, and is a fruitful source of dissensions among the people.

荒れ地は、その土地自身の力で切り拓かれねばならず、貧しさは自らの手で自らを救わねばなりません。 The wilderness must be opened by its own resources, and poverty must be made to rescue itself.

殿には、この飢えた地から道理として見込める年貢だけで満足していただき、それ以上を望まぬようお願いいたします。 Let my Lord be satisfied with the revenue that can be reasonably expected from his famished district, and expect no more from it.

一反の田から二俵の米が穫れたなら、一俵は民の糧に、もう一俵は残る荒れ地を拓く元手にあてるのです。 Should one tan of such a field yield two bags of rice, one bag should go to the sustenance of the people, and the other bag to the fund for the opening-up of the rest of the wilderness.

この豊かな日の本の国も、神代の昔、まさにこの道だけによって切り拓かれました。 In this way alone was this our fruitful Nippon opened to cultivation in the days of the gods.

当時はすべてが荒野でした。何の外からの助けもなく、人々は自らの労苦と土地そのものの力とで、 All was wilderness then; and without any outside help, by their own efforts, with the land's own resources,

今わたしたちが目にするような田畑、庭、道、町を作り上げたのです。 they made fields, gardens, roads, and cities, as we see them now.

仁愛、勤勉、自助——これらの徳を厳しく実行することにこそ、この村々の望みがあります。 Love, diligence, self-help,—in the strict enforcement of these virtues lies the hope of these villages;

今から十年、心を尽くして誠実にこの仕事に取り組めば、必ずや元の繁栄を取り戻せると信じております」 and I should not wonder, if, ten years from this date, with patient application of ourselves in the work with all sincerity, we could bring them back to their original prosperity."

大胆で、理にかない、しかも金のかからぬ策であった。誰がこの案に賛同せずにいられよう。 Bold, reasonable, inexpensive plan! Who will not consent to such a plan?

経済の立て直しにおいて道徳の力をこれほど前面に押し出した村興しの案は、めったに提案されたことがなかった。 Seldom was such a scheme of restoration of villages ever proposed, making moral forces prominent factors in reforms of economic kind.

それは、信仰を経済に応用したものだった。 It was the economic application of Faith.

この男には清教徒の血がいくらか流れていた。いや、むしろ彼は、西洋渡来の「最大幸福」の哲学にまだ穢されていない、生粋の日本人だったのである。 The man had a tincture of Puritanic blood in him; or rather he was a genuine Japanese untainted yet by the Greatest-Happiness-Philosophy of the Occidental importation.

彼はまた、自分の言葉を信じてくれる人々を、まず良き主君その人の中に見いだした。 He also found men who believed in his words, his good Lord the first of all.

「文明」というものは、この百年ほどの間に、私たちをどれほど変えてしまったことだろう。 How did "civilization" change us within a hundred years or so!

この案は採用され、われらが百姓道徳家は、十年の間これらの村の実質的な代官となることとなった。 The plan was adopted, and our peasant-moralist was to be the virtual governor of those villages for ten years.

だが彼は、自らの先祖伝来の地の再興をなかば途上のまま去らねばならぬことを悲しんだ。 But sad was he to leave the restorative work of his ancestral property only half-completed.

彼ほどの熱烈な誠実さを持つ者にとって、全身全霊を傾けぬ関わり方はすべて罪であった。 To a man of his ardent sincerity anything but a whole-souled devotion to any enterprise is sin;

そして今、公の仕事を引き受けるからには、私事はすべて顧みぬこととした。 and now that he undertakes a public work, his private interests are to be wholly disregarded.

「幾千の家を救おうとする者は、自らの家を犠牲にすることでしかそれをなし得ない」と彼は己に言い聞かせた。 "He that would save the homes of thousands can do so only at the expense of his own home," he says to himself.

彼は妻の同意を得て、二人で温めてきた望みを犠牲にし、 He gets his wife's consent to the sacrifice of their cherished hope,

先祖の墓の前ですべての決断を「声に出して」告げ、家のことを整えると、 tells all of his decisions "audibly at his ancestors' graves," finishes up his home,

あたかも別の世界へ向かう者のように、「背後の船をすべて焼き払い」故郷の村を後にし、 and like a man bound for another world, he leaves his native village, "burning all ships behind him,"

主君と国の人々に大胆にも請け合った仕事へと乗り出したのである。 and enters upon the task he so boldly guaranteed to his Lord and countrymen.

荒野との、また人々の頑なな心との「戦い」の子細については、ここでは立ち入らない。 With the details of his "battles with wilderness, and wildness of his people's heart," we will not concern ourselves at present.

彼には術策と呼べるものがほとんどなかった。 Of arts and policies he had scarcely none.

彼の信条は単純で、「ひとつの魂の誠実さは、天と地をも動かすに足る」というものだった。 His simple faith was this, that "the sincerity of a single soul is strong enough to move both heaven and earth."

彼は甘美なものをすべて自らに禁じ、木綿の粗末な着物だけをまとい、 He denied to himself all sweet things, put on nothing but cotton stuffs,

村人の家では決して食事をとらず、一日にわずか二時間しか眠らず、 never ate at his people's houses, slept only two hours a day,

誰よりも先に田に出て、誰もが去るまでそこに残り、 was in the field before any of his men was, remained there till all left,

貧しい村人たちに降りかかる最も過酷な境遇を、自らも耐え忍んだ。 and himself endured the hardest of lots that befell his poor villagers.

彼は自分自身を測るのと同じ物差しで人々を測った——動機の誠実さである。 He judged his men with the same standard with which he judged himself,—the sincerity of motives.

彼にとって最良の働き手とは、最も多く働く者ではなく、最も気高い動機で働く者だった。 With him the best laborer is not he that does most work, but one that works with the noblest motive.

ある男が、三人分の仕事をこなす最も勤勉な働き者、この上なく愛想の良い人物として推挙された。 A man was recommended to him as the hardest worker, one who did three men's work, the most affable fellow, etc.

そうした推挙に対し、われらが百姓代官は長らく無関心であった。 To all such recommendations our peasant-governor was for a long time indifferent;

だが同僚たちから、この「愛想の良い男」への正当な褒賞を求められると、 but when pressed by his associates for the due reward of this "affable fellow,"

尊徳はその男を呼び出し、他の役人の前で見せていたのと同じやり方で、その場で一日分の仕事をやってみせるよう求めた。 Sontok called the man to himself, and required of him to perform the day's labor in his presence in the same way that he was reported to do it before other officers.

男はそれができぬことを認め、居並ぶ役人の目の前で三人分の仕事をこなすふりをした邪な魂胆を、たちどころに白状した。 The man owned his inability to do so, and straightway confessed the sinister motive he had in forcing himself to three-men's labor before the eyes of the attending officials.

代官は自らの経験から、人の力にはおのずと限りがあることを知っており、そのような報告に欺かれることはなかった。 The governor knew by his own experience the limit of a man's capability, and he was not to be deceived by any such report like that.

男は罰せられ、その欺きをたしなめられて田へと送り返された。 The man was punished, and sent back to the field with due admonition for his hypocrisy.

彼の働き手の中にもう一人、一人前にも満たぬ年老いた男がいた。 Another among his laborers was an aged man, hardly equal to one man's capability.

彼はいつも切り株掘りをしていた——骨の折れる、しかもさして目立たぬ仕事である。 He was always found working at stumps,—a toilsome job, not the kind of work that can make much show.

他の者が休んでいるときでさえ、彼はそこで働き続け、自ら選んだその境遇に明らかな満足を見せていた。 There he would work even when others were at rest, with an evident contentment in the lot he chose for himself.

人々は彼を「切り株掘り」と呼び、ほとんど気にも留めなかった。だが代官の目だけは、彼に注がれていた。 "Stump-digger" they called him, and very little notice was taken of him. But the governor's eyes were upon him.

いつものように、働きと分担に応じて各人に裁定が下されるある支払いの日、 On a certain pay-day, when, as was usual with our governor, judgement was passed upon each laborer according to his merit and share in the work,

最高の栄誉と褒賞に呼び出されたのは、ほかならぬこの「切り株掘り」その人であった。 the man who was called up for the highest honor and reward was no other than the "stump-digger" himself,

誰もが、そして誰よりも当人が、大いに驚いた。 to the great astonishment of all, and to none more than to the man himself.

彼には通常の賃金に加え、金十五片(約七十五ドル)が与えられることとなった—— He was to have fifteen pieces of gold (about $75) besides his regular wages,

一日の稼ぎがわずか二セントだった当時としては、途方もない大金であった。 an immense sum of money when a laborer earned only two cents a day.

「お代官様」と老人は声を上げた。「私は歳をとり、他の者に比べれば成し遂げた仕事も遥かに劣ります。一人前の賃金にすら値しません。 "I, my Lord," exclaimed the old man, "am not worthy of even one man's hire, seeing that I am advanced in age, and am far behind others in the work I have accomplished.

何かのお間違いに違いございません。この金は、良心にかけて私のものではございません」 Your Lordship must be mistaken. For conscience's sake, the gold is not mine."

「いや、そうではない」代官は厳かに答えた。「そなたは、誰も好んで働こうとせぬ場所で働いた。 "Not so," gravely remarked the governor. "You worked where nobody else liked to work.

人の目を気にせず、ただ村々への真の奉仕だけを目指してきた。 For men's observations you cared not, and you aimed only at real service to our villages.

そなたが取り除いた切り株は障害を取り払い、私たちの仕事を大いに助けてくれた。 Stumps you removed cleared obstructions, and our work was greatly facilitated thereby.

そなたのような者に報いずして、この先の仕事をどう進めればよいのか。 If I reward not such as you, by what other ways shall I carry on the work that is yet before me.

この贈り物は、そなたの正直に報いる天からのものだ。感謝して受け取り、老後の慰めとするがよい。 The gift is from heaven to reward your honesty. Accept it with thankfulness, and use it to add comforts to your age.

そなたのような正直が認められることほど、私を喜ばせるものはない」 Nothing makes me rejoice more than the recognition of such an honesty as yours."

老人は子供のように泣いた。「袖は絞れるほどに濡れた」という。 The man weeps like a child, "his sleeves wet almost to wringing."

村じゅうがこれに心を打たれた。彼らのもとに、神にも似た者が現れた—— Whole villages are impressed. One godlike has appeared among them,

人知れず行われた徳に、公然と報いる者が。 one who rewardeth openly the virtue that is done in secret.

彼に逆らう者は多くいたが、そのすべてを彼は「仁愛の術」によって和らげていった。 Oppositions he had many, but these he removed by "arts of love."

あるときは、小田原藩主が補佐役として遣わした男を、自分の流儀に馴染ませるのに、三年もの忍耐と辛抱を要した。 Once it took the patience and forbearance of three years to reconcile to him and his ways of doing, a man whom the Lord of Odawara sent as his associate.

村人の一人に、どうしようもない怠け者で、彼のあらゆる方策に激しく反対する男がいた。 One of his villagers was an incorrigible idler, and a vehement opposer to all of his plans.

その男の家は今にも崩れそうな有様で、彼はその貧しさを、新しい統治のふがいなさの何よりの証拠だと隣人たちに言いふらしていた。 The man's house was in a tottering state, and his poverty he would recount to his neighbors as a sure sign of the weakness of the new administration.

たまたま、代官の屋敷のある建物が、この男の堆肥小屋の下にあり、 It happened that a certain [building] of the governor's household was under the man's manure-shed,

長年の手入れ不足で朽ち果て、少し触れただけで崩れ落ちてしまった。 which, by the negligence of many years, was in so rotten a state that a slight touch brought it down to the ground.

男の怒りは収まらなかった。棍棒を手に飛び出し、詫びるその咎人を一撃二撃し、 The man's wrath knew no end. With a club he came out, gave a blow or two to the suppliant transgressor,

主君の屋敷まで追いかけていった。 and pursued after him till he reached his master's house.

代官の門前に立ちはだかると、集まった大勢の前で、 There in the front of the governor's gate the man stood, and recited to the hearing of a large crowd that gathered around him,

自分がどれほどひどい目に遭ったか、そして代官がこの地に平穏をもたらせずにいることをまくし立てた。 the severe ills he suffered, and the governor's inability to give peace and order to the district.

尊徳はその男を自分の前に呼び、この上なく穏やかに、下人の非を詫び、こう続けた。 Sontok ordered the man to his presence, and in the mildest possible way, begged forgiveness for his servant's transgression, and continued:

「そなたの堆肥小屋があれほど脆い有様だったからには、住まいの方もあまり良い状態ではないのではと案じている」 "Seeing that your manure-shed was in so fragile a state, I am afraid your residence also is not in the best of conditions."

「ご承知の通り私は貧しい者です」男はぶっきらぼうに答えた。「家を直す余裕などございません」 "You know I am a poor man," the man bluntly replied, "and I am unable to repair my house."

「なるほど」道徳家は穏やかに答えた。「では、私の方から人をやって直させるというのはどうだろう。そなたはそれを承知してくれるか」 "So," was the gentle answer of the moralist. "How is it then if we send men to repair it for you? Will you give your consent to it?"

驚き、すでに恥じ入る思いに包まれながら、男は答えた。 Taken with surprise, and already a sense of shame coming over him, the man replied,

「そのような有難いお申し出に、私が異を唱えられましょうか。あまりにも過分な情けでございます」 "Could I object to so kind a proposal? That is a mercy too great for me."

男はただちに家へ帰され、古い家を取り壊し、新しい家を建てる地を整えるよう命じられた。 He was at once sent back to his home, to pull down the old house, and to prepare the ground for the erection of the new.

翌日、代官の手の者が万端の支度を整えてやって来て、数週間のうちに、 The next day, the governor's men appeared with all preparations for the new structure, and within a few weeks

近隣でも指折りの立派な家が完成した。 there was finished one of the nicest-looking houses in the whole neighborhood.

堆肥小屋もまた、誰が触れてもびくともせぬよう直された。 The manure-shed also was repaired, so that it could stand any man's touch.

こうして、村一番の荒くれ者も屈服した。それ以来、この男ほど代官に忠実な者はいなくなった。 The worst of the villagers was thus brought down. Ever afterward none remained more faithful to the governor than this man.

彼はのちにその屈辱を語るたび、いつも涙を流したという。 Tears always gushed out when he told afterward of the real humiliation he experienced then.

あるとき、村人の間に不満が広く募り、いかなる「仁愛の術」もそれを鎮められなかった。 Once discontent became general among the villagers, and no "art of love" could subdue it.

われらが代官は、そのすべての責は自分にあると考えた。 Our governor thought he himself was to be blamed for all such.

「私の誠が足りぬゆえに、天がこうして罰を下すのだ」と彼は自らに言い聞かせた。 "Heaven punishes thus my lack in sincerity," he said to himself.

ある日、彼は人々の前から忽然と姿を消し、皆はその行方を案じた。 One day he disappeared suddenly from among his people, and they all became uneasy about his whereabouts.

数日後、彼が遠くの寺に身を寄せ、祈り瞑想していたことがわかった。 Some days after it was found that he had resorted to a distant Buddhist temple, there to pray and to meditate,

だが何より、人々を導くにふさわしい誠を得ようと、二十一日の断食をしていたのである。 but chiefly to fast for one-and-twenty days, that he might be furnished with more sincerity in leading his people.

彼の不在は人々の間に無秩序を意味するとあって、人が遣わされ、一刻も早い帰還を乞うた。 Men were sent thither to entreat him for his speedy return, as his absence meant anarchy among his people, who now had learnt that they could not get along without him.

断食の期間を終えると、彼は軽い食事で体を整え、三週間の絶食明けの翌日には、 The term of his fasting over, he strengthened himself with a slight meal, and the day after his three weeks' abstinence from food,

二十五マイルの道を歩いて村へ戻った。人々が悔い改めたと聞いて、心には喜びが満ちていた。 he walked twenty-five miles to his villages, rejoicing in his heart to hear of the repentance of his people.

この男は、よほど鉄のような体をしていたに違いない。 The man must have had an iron constitution.

幾年もの絶えざる勤勉と倹約、そして何より「仁愛の術」によって、荒野はほぼ姿を消し、 With several years' unabated diligence, economy, and above all, "arts of love," the wilderness had fairly departed,

それなりの実りが戻り始めた。 and something like tolerable productivity began to return.

代官は他国からの移住者を招き入れ、生まれながらの住民よりも手厚く遇した。 The governor invited immigrants from other provinces, and them he treated with more consideration than he did the native-born inhabitants,

「よそ者こそ、私たち自身の子ら以上に、私たちの情けを必要としているのだから」と彼は言った。 "because," said he, "strangers need more kindness from us than our own children."

彼にとって、ある地域の完全な再興とは、土地に肥沃さが戻るというだけの意味ではなく、 To him the complete restoration of any district does not mean the mere return of fertility to the soil,

「十年の凶作にも耐えうるだけの蓄え」を意味した。 but "provisions enough for ten years of scarcity."

そこで彼は、中国の賢人の「九年の蓄えなき国は危うく、三年の蓄えなき国はもはや国とは言えぬ」との言葉を文字通り実践した。 Therein he followed literally the words of a Chinese sage who said, "A country without nine years' provisions is in danger; and that without three years' is no country at all."

この百姓聖人の見方によれば、今日どれほど誇り高い国であっても、それだけでは「国とは言えぬ」ということになる。 According to the views of our peasant-saint, then, the proudest of nations of now-a-days is "no nation at all."

だが、この蓄えが整う前に、飢饉が訪れた。一八三三年は、北東の諸国すべてにとって大きな苦難の年となった。 But famine set in before these provisions were made. The year 1833 was one of great distress to all the north-eastern provinces.

尊徳は、夏に茄子を食べたときにその年の凶作を予見した。 Sontok foretold the year's poor harvest when eating an egg-plant fruit in summer.

その味がまるで秋の茄子のようだった、それは「日輪がすでにその年の光を使い果たした」明らかな徴だと彼は語った。 He said that it tasted very much like that of autumn, an evident sign that "the sun had already spent forth its rays for the year."

彼はただちに、一戸につき一反の割で粟を蒔き、その年の米の不作を補うよう人々に命じた。 He at once gave orders to his people to sow millet at the rate of a tan to a family, so as to supply the deficiency of the rice-crop of the year.

そのとおりに実行され、翌年、近隣諸国が軒並み飢饉に苦しむ中、 This was done; and the year following, when scarcity reigned throughout the neighboring provinces,

尊徳の治める三村では、ただの一軒も食に窮する家はなかった。 not a single family in the three villages under Sontok's supervision suffered from lack.

「誠の道は、先を見通すことができる」——われらが代官は、予言者でもあったのだ。 "The ways of sincerity can know beforehand." Our governor was a prophet as well.

約束の十年が終わるころには、かつて藩内で最も貧しかった土地が、最も秩序ある、最も蓄えの豊かな、 At the end of the promised ten years, the once poorest land in the empire became the most orderly, the best provided,

天然の肥沃さにおいても国内随一の実り豊かな地域となっていた。 and as far as its natural fertility went, the most productive district in the whole country.

村々はかつての繁栄の日々と同じく四千俵の年貢を納められるようになっただけでなく、 Not only were the villages made to yield a revenue of 4,000 bags of rice as in their former days of prosperity,

何年もの凶作に備えられるだけの穀物を蓄えた蔵をいくつも持つに至った。 but they had now several granaries well-filled with substantial grain to provide for many years of scarcity;

さらに嬉しいことに、代官自身も後年慈善のために自由に使える金を数千片手元に残していたという。 and we are glad to add that the governor himself had several thousand pieces of gold left for himself which he was to freely use for philanthropic purposes in after years.

その名声は今や遠く広く伝わり、諸国の領主たちが使者を送っては、自領の荒れ村再興の教えを乞うた。 His fame now spread far and wide, and nobles from all parts of the country sent in messengers to ask his instructions for the restoration of desolated villages in their provinces.

誠実さだけがこれほど際立った成果を生んだ例は、かつてなかった。 Never before had sincerity alone given so prominent a result.

これほど単純で、これほど元手のかからぬ仕方で、一人の人間が天とともにこれほどのことを成し遂げられるのだ。 So simple, so cheap, a man with Heaven alone can accomplish so much.

尊徳のこの最初の公の偉業が、当時の怠惰な世に与えた道徳的な衝撃は、計り知れぬものだった。 The moral impressions of Sontok's first public achievement was tremendous upon the indolent community of his time.

四、個人への助け IV. Individual Helps

彼の郷里への他の公的な奉仕を語る前に、苦しむ同胞たちに差し伸べた個人的な助けについて、ここでいくつか記しておこう。 Before speaking of his other public services to his country, let me narrate here something of the friendly help he was called upon to offer to his suffering fellowmen.

彼自身まったくの独学独力の人であった彼は、勤勉と真心さえあれば、独立と自尊にたどり着けぬ者などいないと信じていた。 Himself a wholly self-made man, he knew of no case which industry and sincerity of heart could not bring up to independence and self-respect.

「この宇宙は絶えず動き続け、その周りのあらゆるものの成長に、止まるということがない。 "The universe moves on and on, and a stop there is not in the growth of all things around us.

もし人がこの永遠の成長という法則に従い、働くことをやめずにいるなら、貧しさなど、たとえ求めても得られはしない」 If but a man conforms himself to this law of everlasting growth, and with it ceases not to work, poverty, though he seeks it, is impossible."

彼はこう、貧困にあえぐ百姓の一団に語った。彼らは領主の悪政を嘆き、先祖代々の家を捨てようとしていたが、 So he said to a group of poverty-stricken farmers, who, complaining of the misgovernment of their feudal lord, were on the point of leaving their ancestral homes,

その導きと教えを求めて尊徳のもとを訪れたのである。 and came to Sontok for his guidance and instructions.

「そなたたちひとりひとりに手鋤を与えよう」と彼は続けた。 "A hand-plough shall I give each of you," he continued,

「私のやり方を受け入れ、それを守り通すなら、それだけで、荒れた田畑を楽土に変え、負債をすべて返し、 "and if you adopt my way, and abide by it, I assure you, with it you can make a paradise out of your desolated field, pay back all your debts,

よそに幸運を求めることなく、再び豊かさの中で喜べるようになると、私は請け合う」 and can rejoice once more in plenty, without seeking fortune outside of your own land."

男たちはその通りにし、聖者の手から「手鋤ひとつずつ」を受け取り、彼の助言通りに懸命に働き、 The men did so, accepted "a hand-plough each" from the saint's hand, went earnestly to work as he advised,

数年のうちに、失ったものすべてを、それ以上に取り戻した。 and in a few years got back all they had lost and more.

村人への影響力をすっかり失った村長が、知恵を求めて尊徳のもとを訪れた。 A village-mayor who had entirely lost his influence with his people came to Sontok for his wisdom.

聖者の答えは、この上なく単純なものだった。「そなたの中に、自己愛が強すぎるからだ」と彼は言った。 The saint's answer was the simplest that could be imagined. "Because love of self is strong in you," he said.

「利己心は獣のものだ。利己的な人間は、獣の同類にすぎぬ。 "Selfishness is of beasts; and a selfish man is of beast-kind.

そなたが人々に影響を及ぼせるのは、自分自身とその持てるものすべてを、彼らに差し出したときだけだ」 You can have influence over your people only by giving yourself and your all to them."

「どうすればそれができましょうか」と村長は尋ねた。 "How can I do so?" the mayor inquired.

「そなたの土地を、家を、衣を、その全部を売り」尊徳は答えた。 "Sell your land, your house, your raiment, your all," was Sontok's reply,

「それで得た金はことごとく村の基金に納め、そなた自身を丸ごと人々への奉仕に捧げるのだ」 "and whatever money you get thereby contribute to the village fund, and give yourself wholly to the service of the people."

これほど厳しいやり方に、たやすく身を投じられる者はいない。村長は決断までに数日の猶予を求めた。 No natural man can easily commit himself to so severe a procedure like this. The mayor asked for several days' delay before he could give his decision.

その犠牲があまりに大きすぎると聞くと、尊徳は言った。「そなたは家族が飢えることを恐れているのだろう。 When told that the sacrifice was altogether too much for him, Sontok said: "I suppose you are afraid of the starvation of your family.

そなたが己の分を果たすなら、この私が、己の分を果たす術を知らぬとでも思うのか」 Think you that if you do your part, I, your adviser, know not how to do my part?"

男は戻り、教えられた通りにした。彼の影響力と人望はたちどころに戻った。 The man returned, and did as was instructed. His influence and popularity returned at once.

しばらくの間、彼の欠乏は敬愛する師が自らの蓄えから補ったが、まもなく村全体が村長を支えるようになり、 His lack for a time his revered instructor supplied out of his own store; but soon the whole village came to the mayor's support,

さほど経たぬうちに、彼は以前よりも裕福な身となっていた。 and within a short period, he was a wealthier man than before.

藤沢の町のある米商人は、凶作の年に高値で穀物を売って相当な財を成していたが、 A rice-merchant in the township of Fujisawa, who had made a considerable fortune by selling his grain at high prices in a year of scarcity,

相次ぐ不幸に見舞われ、破産の淵に立たされた。 came very near to bankruptcy by successive misfortunes that befell his family.

その親戚の一人が尊徳と親しい間柄だったため、失った財産を取り戻す手立てを聖者の知恵に求めた。 A relative of his was an intimate acquaintance of Sontok, and the saint's wisdom was asked to devise some means for the restoration of the lost property.

私利をもくろむ者との相談にはいつも気が進まなかった彼だが、長く懇願されて、ようやくそれに応じた。 Always very reluctant to confer with the people who had personal interests in view, he yielded to their request only after long importunity.

その男を道徳の目で診断すると、悩みの唯一の原因がたちどころに見えてきた。 His moral diagnosis of the man revealed to him at once the sole cause of the trouble.

「今残っているものをすべて施し、素手で一からやり直すことです」と尊徳は言った。 "The way is to give in charity all you have left now," Sontok said, "and to begin anew with your bare hand."

彼の目には、不正に得た財産などは財産のうちに入らなかった。 To his eye, ill-gotten fortune was no fortune at all.

あるものが真に自分のものであるのは、天の正しい理に従って、天から直接それを受け取ったときだけである。 A thing is ours only as we have it directly from Nature by conforming ourselves to her righteous laws.

その男が財産を失ったのは、それがもともと彼のものではなかったからであり、残ったものもまた「穢れて」いた。だから、それにもどうすることもできなかった。 The man lost his property because it had not been originally his, and that which he had left was also "unclean," and hence nothing could be done with it also.

強欲というものは、長く苦しい葛藤なしに、そうたやすくこの根本からの改革に屈するものではない。 Avarice cannot be made to yield itself to such a radical reform without long and painful struggles.

だが、この道徳の医師の評判はあまりに大きく、その処方の効き目を疑うことはできなかった。 But the reputation of the moral-physician was too great to doubt the efficacy of his prescription,

その助言は実行に移され、友人・親戚一同を驚かせ、いや、狼狽させたとさえ言ってよいだろう。 and his advice was followed to the amazement, and (may we say) consternation of all his friends and relatives.

男は残っていたもの、金にして七百片(三千五百ドル)を町の人々に分け与え、 The man distributed all he had left, amounting to 700 pieces of gold ($3,500) among his townsmen,

自らは、少年の頃から知る唯一の「素手」の稼業である船漕ぎに身を投じた。 and he himself went to rowing, the only "bare-hand" trade he was acquainted with from his boyhood.

この決断が、当人にも、町の人々全体にも、どれほどの道徳的な影響を与えたか、想像に難くない。 We can easily imagine the moral effect of such a decision on the man's part both upon the man himself, and upon the townsmen at large.

彼の強欲に対する恨みはたちまち消え去り、彼の不幸を喜んでいた者たちまでもが助けの手を差し伸べるようになり、 All the bitterness against him caused by his avarice was removed at once, and those who rejoiced in his misfortunes now came to his help,

彼が船を漕いだのはごく短い間だけだった。 and he was upon his oars only for a very short time.

今度は町の人々すべての好意とともに、幸運が彼に微笑みかけ始め、その晩年は、始まりよりもさらに栄えたと伝えられている。 Fortune began to smile upon him, this time with the good-will of all his townsmen, and we are told that his latter end was more prosperous than his beginning.

ただ残念なことに、年を取ると強欲がまた彼のもとに戻り、その最期は困窮のうちに過ぎたという。 Only we are sorry to hear that with age avarice returned to him once more, and his last days were spent in penury.

孔子の書にこうあるではないか。「禍福は自ら求めざるはなし」と。 Does not a book of Confucius say, "Misery and happiness come not by themselves; only men invite them?"

われらが師は、たやすく近づける人物ではなかった。 Our teacher was not an easy man to approach.

身分の如何を問わず、見知らぬ客はいつも門前で「取り込み中」という決まり文句で追い返された。 Strangers of whatever ranks were always repulsed at his gate with the customary oriental excuse "I am pressed with duty."

よほど粘り強い者だけが、彼に会うことができた。 Only the most importunate could get a hearing from him.

訪ねてきた者の忍耐が尽きそうになると、師は「その者を助けるべき時は、まだ来ていない」と言うのだった。 Should the inquirer's patience fail, the teacher would say "My time of helping him is not come yet."

あるとき、檀家の窮乏を救う教えを乞うために遠路はるばる歩いてきた一人の僧が、にべもなく面会を拒まれたという。 Once we are told that a Buddhist priest, who came a long distance walking to get instruction for the relief of his parishioners, was bluntly refused audience;

しかし辛抱強いこの僧は、師の家の前の地面に自らの衣を広げ、 but he a patient man spread his garment upon the ground in front of the teacher's house,

そこに三日三晩座り続けた。行と粘り強さによって、師が会ってくれるようになると信じていたのである。 and there for three days and nights he sat, believing that by penance and pertinacity, the teacher might be induced to give him a hearing.

だが尊徳は、「犬のように」「乞食坊主」が門のそばに座っていると聞いて、ひどく腹を立て、 But Sontok was extremely wroth to hear that "dog-like" a "beggar-priest" sat near his gate,

すぐに立ち去り、「人々の魂のために祈り、断食するがよい」と命じた。 and he ordered him to begone at once and "pray and fast for people's souls."

こうした仕打ちが幾度か繰り返された後、ようやく彼はこの僧を心を開いて迎え入れ、 Such a treatment was repeated several times before he received the priest in confidence,

この僧こそ、後年、彼の金と知恵と友情とを惜しみなく受ける者となったのである。 and this was he, who, in after years, was to be a free recipient of his gold, wisdom, and friendship.

彼の友情は、いつも得るのに大きな代償を要したが、ひとたび得られれば、これほど尊く長く続くものもなかった。 His friendship was always very costly to get, but when once procured, nothing was so precious and lasting.

偽りある不誠実な人間に対しては、彼にもなす術がなかった。 He could do nothing with false insincere men.

宇宙とその法則そのものがそうした人間に敵対しており、彼の力をもってしても、いかなる人の力をもってしても、 The universe and its laws were against such men, and nothing in his power or any man's power

彼らを不幸と堕落から救い出すことはできなかった。 could rescue them from misery and degradation.

そうした者に対しては、まず「天地の理」に立ち返らせ、 Them he would first reconcile with the "Reason of Heaven and Earth,"

その上で、真に必要な人としての助けを施した。 and then administer to them whatever human helps that might be absolutely necessary.

「胡瓜を植えて、胡瓜の実り以外の何かを得られると思うな。人が蒔いたものを、人はまた刈り取らねばならぬ」 "Think not you can get anything else than cucumber-fruit when you plant cucumber. The thing a man planteth, the same he must reap also."

「誠だけが不幸を幸福に変える。術策も策略も、何の役にも立たぬ」 "Sincerity alone can turn misery into happiness; arts and policies avail nothing."

「ひとつの魂は、この宇宙にあってはあまりに小さきものだ。だがその誠は、天と地を動かす」 "An individual soul is an infinitesimally small thing in the universe, but its sincerity can move heaven and earth."

「務めは、その結果のいかんにかかわらず、務めである」 "Duty is duty irrespective of its result."

こうした、そしてこれに類する数々の教えによって、彼は導きと救いを求めてやって来た幾多の苦しむ魂を助けた。 Such and many like them are the precepts with which he helped out many a struggling soul that came to him for guidance and deliverance.

こうして彼は自然と人との間に立ち、道を外れたことでその恵みを失った者たちを、自然のもとへと連れ戻していったのである。 Thus he stood between nature and man, restoring to the former them, who, through their moral obliquity, had forfeited the right she so freely bestowed upon them.

この地にこのところ溢れかえる西洋のあらゆる知恵も、この、われらと同じ血と肉を分けた者による福音に比べれば、いったい何だというのだろう。 What are all the wisdoms of the West that have recently flooded our soil, in comparison with an evangel such as this, of our own kin and blood!

五、より広く国のために V. Public Service at Large

下野国の三つの荒れ村を再興したことで彼の信念は現実に証明され、その名声は疑いようもなく確立した。 His faith once worked out in the restoration of the three deserted villages in the province of Shimotsuke, and his fame thus indubitably established,

こうして彼は、国じゅうの諸侯から絶え間なく訪ねられる身となった。 he became an object of constant interruption by nobles from all parts of the country.

彼はいつもの無愛想なやり方で来訪者を迎えることでこうした押しかけを防いだが、 He fenced himself against such intrusions by his usual blunt ways of receiving his visitors,

その「信の試練」に耐えた者は少なくなく、そうした者たちは皆、彼の賢明な助言と実際の助けの恩恵にあずかった。 but such as endured his "test of faith" were not few, and these had all the benefit of his wise councils and practical help.

生涯のうちに、広大な領地を持つ十人ほどの諸侯が、困窮した領内の改善のために彼の力を借り、 During his life-time, some half-a-score nobles representing a wide extent of land had his services in improvement of their impoverished dominions,

恩恵にあずかった村の数は数え切れぬほどだった。 and the number of villages likewise benefited was innumerable.

晩年には国への貢献があまりに大きくなり、幕府に登用されるまでになったが、 Near the end of his life his service to the nation became so invaluable that he was employed by the Central Government;

その素朴な使命の性質ゆえに、彼が最も本領を発揮したのは、士族の格式や社交上のしきたりに縛られぬ、自らと同じ貧しい労働者たちの間にいるときだった。 but the homely nature of his mission made him appear at his best when he was among his own class of poor laborers, unhampered by the official and social conventionalities of the titled classes.

だが不思議なのは、この最も卑しい生まれの、この上なく素朴な教養しか持たぬ百姓が、 The wonder is, however, that he a peasant of the meanest birth and the simplest culture

「高き地位の人々」と交わるときには、まるで「真の貴人」のように己を処することができたということである。 could have managed himself like a "real noble" when associating with "men in high places."

当然ながら、その恩恵に最も多く浴したのは、彼自身の主君である小田原藩主であった。 Naturally his own Lord of Odawara was to get most from him.

同名の城下町に付属する広大な領地が彼の監督下に置かれ、 The large dominion attached to the castle-town of the same name was placed under his supervision,

その荒れ果てた地の多くが、彼の飽くなき勤勉と尽きぬ「仁愛の術」によって回復した。 and much of the desolated and waste places in it was recovered by his tireless industry and never-failing "arts of love."

一八三六年の大飢饉は、彼が同胞に施した最も際立った奉仕のひとつを見せる場となった。 The great famine of 1836 witnessed one of his most signal services to his fellowmen.

幾千もの人々が飢えで死に瀕していたとき、彼は(当時江戸にいた)主君の命により、その速やかな救済にあたることとなった。 When thousands of people were on the point of death from starvation, he was commissioned by his Lord (then residing in Yedo) to undertake their speedy relief.

尊徳は、当時なら丸二日はかかる道のりを急いで小田原へ向かい、 Sontok hastened to Odawara, then a journey of two full days,

飢えた人々を今すぐ救うために城の蔵を開ける鍵を渡すよう、当地の役人たちに求めた。 and asked the men in authority there to hand him the key to open the castle-granary for the immediate relief of the starving people.

「殿の書付が届くまでは」というのが、いささか見下したような彼らの返事だった。 "Not till we have the Lord's written permission," was their rather contemptuous answer.

「わかりました」尊徳は答えた。「ですが皆様、書付が届くまでの間にも、 "All right, then," Sontok responded. "But, gentlemen, seeing that during the interval between this and the arrival of the written permission of the Lord

さらに多くの飢えた民が命を落とすことでしょう。ならば私たちも彼らの忠実な守り手として、 many more of our starving people shall die of hunger, I believe it behooves us as their faithful guardians

今の彼らと同じように食を断ち、使いの者が戻るまでこの部屋で断食して待つのが道理と存じます。 that we should abstain from food as they are now doing, and should stay here in this office-room fasting till the return of your messenger.

そうすれば、私たちも民の苦しみの一端を知ることができましょう」 Thus we may learn somewhat of the nature of our people's suffering."

四日の断食など、この役人たちにはとても耐えられそうになかった。鍵はただちに尊徳に渡され、救済はすぐさま実行された。 Four day's fasting was too horrible to think of to these officers. The key was instantly delivered to Sontok, and the relief was effected at once.

いずこの時代いずこの地であれ、飢えが民の戸口に迫り、役所仕事が無用な形式を踏まねば救いをもたらせずにいるとき、すべての民の守り手たちが、この道徳家の提案を心に留めてくれればと願わずにいられない。 Would that all guardians of people of whatever clime at whatever time may be mindful of our moralist's proposal when hunger waits at the people's door, and officialism must go through useless formalities before it can bring relief to the sufferers!

この機会に、彼は「手立てなき折の飢饉救済の道」と題した、かの有名な講話を行った。 It was upon this occasion that he delivered his famous discourse upon "the Ways of Famine-Relief in default of Means for that End."

主な聞き手は、主君によって領国統治の長に任じられた代官であった。 His chief audience was the governor of the dominion appointed by his Lord as the chief executive of the provincial government.

ここに、その講話の一部を、彼という人間をよく表す例として引いておこう。 We give here some fragments of the discourse, as it is very characteristic of the man who gave it.

「国が飢え、蔵は空となり、民に食べるものがない——これは、統治する者自身の咎でなくて何であろうか。 "That the land famishes, the granaries are empty, and the people have nothing to eat,—whose blame is this but that of the Ruler himself?

彼は天の子らを託されているのではないか。彼らを善へと導き、悪から遠ざけ、 Is he not intrusted with Heaven's children, and is it not his mission to lead them into good and away from evil,

平安のうちに生き、留まらせることこそ、彼の使命ではないか。 and so enable them to live and abide in peace?

この務めのために、彼は十分な俸禄を得、家族を養い、その身は安泰である。 For this service which is expected from him he is paid abundantly, and he brings up his family, and they are safe.

だが今、民が飢えに追いやられていながら、彼はそれを自分の責めとは思っていない。 But now that his people are reduced to hunger, he thinks not himself responsible for it:—

皆さん、私はこれほど嘆かわしいことを、天の下に他に知りません。 Gentlemen, I know of nothing under heaven so lamentable as this.

この折、もし彼が何らかの救済の手立てを講じられたなら、それでよい。だがそれがかなわぬなら、統治する者は天の前に己の罪を告白し、 At this time, should he succeed in devising some means of relief, well; but if not, the Ruler should confess his sin before Heaven,

自ら進んで食を断ち、死ぬべきです。 and himself go to voluntary fasting and die!

そしてその下の役人たち——郡の役人、続いて村の役人たちも、また食を断って死ぬべきです。 Then his sub-officers,—county-officers after him, and then village-officers,—they also should abstain from food and die,

彼らもまた、務めを怠り、民に死と苦しみをもたらした責めを負うのですから。 for they too have neglected their duty and brought death and suffering upon the people.

こうした犠牲が、飢えた民の心に与える道徳的な効果は、たちまち明らかになるでしょう。 The moral effect of such sacrifices upon the famished people will be evident at once.

彼らは互いにこう言い合うようになるでしょう。『代官様も、その下の役人様たちも、 They will now say among themselves, 'The governor and his sub-officers held themselves responsible for the distress that is upon us

自分たちには何の落ち度もないのに、この苦難の責めを自ら負われた。 when they have really nothing to blame themselves with.

飢えているのは、私たち自身の不用意さ、豊かな時の贅沢と浪費のせいなのだ。 Starvation is upon us because of our own improvidence, luxury, and extravagance in times of abundance.

私たちは、敬愛する役人様たちのこの嘆かわしい最期に責めを負うべきであり、 We are accountable for the lamentable end of our honored officers,

今こうして飢えて死ぬのも、まったく当然のことだ』と。 and that we should now die of hunger is entirely proper.'

こうして飢えへの恐れは去り、それとともに死への恐れも去るのです。彼らの心は今、安らいでいる。 Thus the fear of famine shall depart, and with it the fear of death also. Their mind is now at peace.

恐れさえ去れば、食の蓄えは手の届くところにある。 Fear once gone, abundance of food-supply is within their reach.

富める者はその持てるものを貧しき者と分かち合い、あるいは人々は山に登り、葉や根を食んでもよい。 The rich may share his possessions with the poor; or they may climb mountains, and feed upon leaves and roots.

たった一年の凶作が、この国の米や粟をすべて食い尽くすことなどありえず、山にも丘にも、青々としたものはいくらでもある。 A single year's famine cannot exhaust all the rice and millet of the land, and hills and mountains have their supply of green things.

国が飢えるのは、恐れが民の心を支配し、食を求める気力を奪い、死に至らしめるからです。 The nation famishes because Fear reigns dominant in the people's mind, and depriving them of energy to seek food, causes them to die.

弾を込めぬ銃の音だけで臆病な鳥が落ちることがあるように、凶作の年には、人々は飢えという響きだけに驚き、死んでいくのです。 As guns fired without shots often bring down timid birds, so men in years of scarcity are astounded with the sound of hunger, and die.

それゆえ、まず民の指導者たちが進んで飢え、死ぬべきです。そうすれば飢えへの恐れは民の心から消え去り、 Therefore let the leaders of the people die first of voluntary starvation, and the fear of hunger shall be dissipated from the people's heart,

皆が満たされ、救われるでしょう。 and they all shall be filled and saved.

郡や村の役人たちの犠牲を待たずとも、目指す結果は得られると私は信じます。 I do not believe you need wait for the sacrifice of your country- and village-officers before you realize the result you aim at.

代官様おひとりの犠牲だけで、その目的には十分だと思います。 I believe the sacrifice of the governor alone is sufficient for this purpose.

皆様、これこそ、飢えた民に与えるものが何も残っていないときに、彼らを救うひとつの道なのです」 This, gentlemen, is one way of saving your hungry people when you have nothing left to give them for their relief."

講話は終わった。代官は恥じ入り、うろたえた様子で、長い沈黙の後にこう言った。「あなたの論に、返す言葉もありません」 The discourse ended. The governor in shame and dismay, said after a long silence, 'I should say it is impossible to gainsay your argument.'

この皮肉は、真剣に語られたものではあったが、もちろん文字通りに実行するつもりで言われたわけではなかった。 The sarcasm, though seriously spoken, was not of course intended to be carried into practice.

救済は、彼の他のすべての仕事を特徴づけていたのと同じ簡素さ—— The relief was effected with the same simplicity as that which characterized all his other labors,

速やかさ、勤勉、苦しむ者への深い共感、そして自然とその恵み深い法則への信頼——によって行われた。 promptness, diligence, intense sympathy with the sufferer, and trust in Nature and her beneficent laws.

穀物と金が、苦しむ百姓たちに貸し与えられ、五年のうちに収穫によって分割で返済されることとなった。 Grain and money were loaned to the suffering farmers, to be paid back in instalment within five years by crops;

そして、こうして救われた素朴な百姓たちの名誉のために、また救いが差し伸べられたその誠実さの証しとして記しておきたいのは、 and be it mentioned in honor of the simple-hearted peasants thus succored, as well as of the good faith in which the succor was offered,

その約束は誠実に、そして自ら進んで守られ、救われた四万三百九十人の中、期限に至って返済不能となった者はただの一人もいなかったということである。 that the promise was faithfully and willingly kept, not one of the 40,390 sufferers so relieved proving himself insolvent at the end of the stipulated term!

天の理と手を携える者は、急がない。また、目の前のことのためだけに計画し、働くこともない。 He that is in league with Nature hastes not; neither does he plan and work for the present alone.

彼はいわば自らを自然の流れの中に置き、それを助け、高めながら、自らもまたそれによって助けられ、前へと進められていく。 He places himself in Nature's current, as it were, and helps and enhances it, and is himself helped and forwarded thereby.

宇宙を背に負う者にとって、仕事の大きさは彼を驚かせはしない。 With the universe at his back, the magnitude of the work astounds him not.

「すべての物事には、自然の道筋というものがある」と尊徳はよく言った。「私たちはその自然の道を探し出し、それに自らを従わせねばならない。 "There are natural courses for all things," Sontok used to say, "and we are to seek out Nature's ways and to conform ourselves thereto.

そうすれば、山を平らにし、海を干し、大地そのものをも私たちの用に役立てることができる」 Thus can mountains be levelled and seas be drained, and the earth itself be made to serve our purpose."

あるとき彼は、利根川下流の大湿地を干拓するいくつかの案について、政府に報告するよう命じられた。 Once he was appointed by his government to report upon some possible plans of draining the great marshes on the lower course of the river Tone.

もし成し遂げられれば、この事業は計り知れぬ公益をもたらす三つの目的を果たすことになる。 If accomplished, such an enterprise would serve triple purposes of inestimable public benefit:

浅く瘴気の漂う湿地から幾千エーカーもの肥沃な土地を取り戻し、洪水時には余分な水を排出して毎年の被害を大きく減らし、 it would recover thousands of acres of fertile land from the shallow and miasmatic marsh; would drain off surplus water in time of flood, and obviate much of damage yearly done in those quarters;

さらに、その川と江戸湾との間に新たな近道を開くことになる。 and would afford a new and short passage between the river and the bay of Yedo.

切り開くべき距離は、湿地から湾まで十マイル、その湿地の二つの主要な部分の間が五マイル—— The distance to be cut is ten miles between the marsh and the bay, and five miles between the two main sections of the former,

合わせて十五マイルにわたり、泥山と砂地を掘り抜かねばならない。 in all, fifteen miles of excavation through mud-hills and sand.

この試みは一度ならず行われながら、そのたびに絶望のうちに放棄されてきた。 The attempt has been made more than once, only to be given up in despair;

そしてこの仕事は今もなお、これを成し遂げる日本のレセップスとも言うべき、傑出した頭脳の出現を待っている。 and the work is still there waiting for some master mind,—a Japanese Lesseps—to carry it into completion.

この壮大な事業についての尊徳の報告は、いささか謎めいたものだったが、 Sontok's report upon this gigantic enterprise was rather enigmatic;

これほどの規模の土木事業の多くが座礁してきた、まさにその急所を突いていた。 but it hit the point upon which many an engineer-ing work of like magnitude made shipwreck.

「可能であり、また不可能である」と報告は述べた。「自然な、そして唯一可能な道筋が採られ、それに従われるなら、可能である。 "Possible, yet impossible," said the report: "Possible, if the natural and only possible course be adopted and followed;

だが、人間というものは概してそのような道筋に従うのを嫌うものであるから、不可能でもある。 but impossible, because human nature in general is loathe to follow such a course.

運河が掘られることになるその地域の民の道徳の荒廃が、私には見える。それはまず「仁愛の術」によって正されねばならず、それこそが着手すべき仕事の不可欠な前提である。 I see the demoralization of the people of the district through which the canal is to be dug, and that must be righted first by 'the arts of love' as the essential preliminary to the work to be undertaken.

そのような人々の間にばら撒かれる金は、実際にどれほどの仕事が成し遂げられるかは措くとしても、彼らに悪しき影響しか及ぼさない。 Money spent among such people cannot but have vicious effects upon them, to say nothing of the amount of actual work accomplished thereby.

だが、この事業の性質からすれば、金にも権威にも、大して多くを期待することはできない。 But the nature of the undertaking under review is such that little can be expected from either money or authority.

強い感謝の念に突き動かされた、心を一つにした民だけが、これを成し遂げられる。 Only a united people impelled by a strong sense of gratitude can do it.

であれば政府は、彼らに『仁愛の術』を施し、その寡婦を慰め、その孤児を庇護し、今の荒廃した民を徳ある民へと変えるべきである。 Let the government therefore apply 'the arts of love' upon them, comfort their widows, shelter their orphans, and make a virtuous people out of the present demoralized population.

ひとたび彼らの誠を呼び覚ませば、山を穿ち岩を砕く仕事も、思いのままになるだろう。 Once you have called forth their sincerity, the boring of mountains and breaking of rocks will be according to your wish.

この道は迂遠に見えるかもしれないが、実は最も短く、最も確かな道なのだ。 The way may look tortuous, but it is the shortest and most effective one.

草木の根には、その花も実もすべてが宿っているではないか。 Does not the root of a plant contain all its flowerage and fruitage?

道徳が先、仕事はその後である。この順序を逆にすることはできない」 Morality first, then work;—you cannot place the latter before the former."

今日の読者の多くは、これほど夢想的な案を退けた当時の政府に同情するかもしれない。 Most of the present-day readers may sympathize with his government that rejected so visionary a plan as this;

だが「パナマの醜聞」を目にした者で、あの壮大な事業が失敗した主な原因が、財政ではなく道徳にあったと気づかぬ者があろうか。 but who has watched the "Panama scandals" and fails to see that the main cause of the failure of that gigantic enterprise was moral and not financial?

コロンとパナマを、まさしく盗人の巣窟に変えてしまったあの黄金は、今もがらくたのようにそこに埋もれたままであり、 The gold that turned Colon and Panama into veritable dens of thieves lies buried there like so much rubbish,

実際のところ、二つの大洋は、地峡の最初のひとすくいの土が取り除かれたときと同じくらい、今も互いに隔たったままなのだ。 and to all practical purposes, the two oceans are as yet as far apart from each other as when the first shovel of dirt was removed from the isthmus.

あのフランスの偉大な技師が、この日本の百姓ほどの道徳的な先見を持ち合わせ、 Would that the great French engineer had possessed something of the moral foresight of the Japanese peasant;

六億もの金をすべて工事そのものに注ぎ込むのではなく、その一部を「仁愛の術」を通じて人の魂に投じていたなら—— and instead of disbursing his six hundred millions wholly upon the work itself, had a part of it invested in human souls through "arts of love;"

レセップスの白髪を飾るのは、ひとつの不面目な失敗がもうひとつの輝かしい成功を覆い隠す姿ではなく、二つの運河であったろうと、誰が疑うだろうか。 then who doubts that Lesseps would have had two canals to crown his gray hairs, instead of the disgraceful failure of one covering up the glorious success of the other?

金にはできることが多いが、徳にはさらに多くのことができる。 Money can do much, but virtue more;

運河建設の計画を立てるにあたって道徳的な要素を勘定に入れる者は、結局のところ、決して最も非実際的な人間ではないのである。 and he who takes into account moral elements in forming his plan for canal construction is not after all the most unpractical of men.

尊徳が生涯に実際に成し遂げた事業の地理的な広がりは、当時の厳しい身分の隔ての中で彼の身分の者としては相当なものではあったが、決して大きくはなかった。 The geographical extent of Sontok's actual accomplishment in his life-time was not large, though considerable for a man of his social position at a time of rigid class distinctions.

彼のあらゆる功績の中でも群を抜いて大きかったのは、今の磐城国にあたる相馬地方の再興であった—— By far the most considerable of all his achievements was the restoration of the Soma region in the present province of Iwaki,

それ自体、二百三十もの村を擁する決して小さくない地域で、今ではこの国でも最も豊かで栄えた地の一つとなっている。 itself a no mean district of two hundred and thirty villages, now one of the wealthiest and most prosperous in the country.

どれほど大きな仕事に臨むときも、彼のやり方はまったく単純だった。 The way he set himself to work in any work of magnitude was perfectly simple.

まず、たいていはその地域で最も貧しい一つの村に全精力を集中し、 He would first concentrate his whole energy upon one typical village,—usually the poorest in the district,

ひたすらな勤勉の力によって、それを自らのやり方に変えていく。 and by sheer dint of industry would convert it into his ways.

これが、仕事全体の中で通常もっとも困難な部分である。 This is usually the hardest part of the whole business.

最初にその一村を救い出せば、そこを足がかりとして地域全体の変革に取りかかることができる。 The one village first rescued, he had as a base to start from for the conversion of the whole district.

彼はいつも、教え導いた百姓たちの間に一種の伝道の精神を吹き込み、 He always infused a kind of missionary spirit among his peasant-converts,

自分たちが師に助けられたのと同じように、隣村を助けるよう求めた。 who were required to help their neighboring villages as they themselves were helped by their teacher.

目の前に示された際立った実例と、新たな感化を受けた人々による惜しみない助けとによって、 With a striking example furnished before their very eyes, and with help freely afforded by men under the new inspiration,

地域全体が同じやり方を採り入れるようになり、単純な伝播の法則によって変革が成し遂げられた。 the whole district was brought to adopt the same method, and conversion was effected by a simple law of propagation.

「一つの村を救える方法は、国全体をも救える。その原理はまったく同じだ」と彼は問う人々によく語った。 "The method that can rescue a village can rescue the whole country; the principle is just the same," he used to say to his inquirers.

「この一つの仕事に、私たちは献身的に取り組もう。その実例が、いずれ国全体を救う助けになるかもしれないのだから」 "Let us apply ourselves devotedly to this one piece of work; for the example may serve to save the whole nation in times to come,"

日光の地域にあるいくつかの荒れ村の再興計画を練りながら、彼は弟子たちにそう語った。 he observed to his disciples while preparing plans for the restoration of a few desolated villages in the Nikko district.

この男は、自らが宇宙の永遠の法則を身につけていることを自覚しており、 The man was conscious of his possession of the everlasting laws of the universe,

彼にとって、挑むにはあまりに難しい仕事も、全身全霊を捧げるにはあまりに易しすぎる仕事も、なかった。 and no work was too difficult for him to attempt, nor too easy to require his whole-souled devotion to it.

当然ながら彼は、その生涯の終わりに至るまで、勤勉に働き続ける人であった。 Naturally he was a hard-working man till the very close of his life.

遠い未来をも見据えて計画し働いた彼であったから、その仕事と感化は、今もなお私たちとともに生きている。 As he planned and worked for the distant future as well, so his works and influence still live with us.

彼自身の手によって再興された、微笑むような村々の数々は、その知恵と、その計画の永続性を今に証ししている。 Many a smiling village of his own reconstruction witnesses to his wisdom and the permanence of his plans;

そして帝国の各地には今なお、この人物の名と教えに結ばれた農民たちの結社が見出され、 while scattered through different parts of the empire are to be found societies of farmers bound by the name and teaching of this man,

彼が意気消沈した労苦の子らに示したその精神を、今に伝え続けているのである。 to perpetuate the spirit he made known to the disheartened sons of toil.
